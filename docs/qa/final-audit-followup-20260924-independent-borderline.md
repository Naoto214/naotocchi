# Independent visual opinion: five selected cases

Reviewed 2026-09-24. Opened the five supplied PNG sheets directly: `cases/139.png`, `318.png`, `336.png`, `025.png`, and `027.png`. Each sheet presents 64/80/104 px at 0/390/1300/1690 ms, with the stated character → expression accent → sweat order. This is visual review of those offline reconstructions, not a browser capture, continuous animation review, or new geometry audit. No static/reduced-motion supplemental sheet was provided or assessed. No repository file was changed.

Acceptance rule used: geometric contact alone is allowed. A confirmed candidate needs clear visual obstruction or a misleading symbol reading. `needs_user_judgment` below means the supplied frames leave a readability concern, but I cannot independently establish a defect that passes that threshold; it is not a recommendation to modify the images or placement automatically.

| Case | Finding | Classification |
|---|---|---|
| #139 — cicada/03 strained | The left sweat drop sits directly over the short gray strained zigzag. In the 0/390 ms samples, especially 64 px, I perceive a green drop with short gray ends more readily than a distinct complete zigzag. Because the mark is near the insect's upper appendages, those gray remnants could be read as a tiny extra antenna-like detail or an attached stroke. However, this possible reading is not compelling enough to call an actual wrong-symbol interpretation. The insect face, head outline and original appendages stay legible; more of the zigzag is visible at 1300/1690 ms and at larger sizes. | **needs_user_judgment** |
| #318 — god/05 strained | The same short zigzag/drop overlap is readily visible in the upper-left of the halo. At 0/390 ms the sweat hides the central bend, leaving a gray slanted stub/cap behind the green drop; at 64 px the separate strained-mark identity is weak. It does not obscure the face or halo, and I do not see it turning into another recognizable state symbol. At 1300/1690 ms the exposed gray form is easier to distinguish. This is a compact accent-readability concern, not demonstrated character obstruction. | **needs_user_judgment** |
| #336 — star/05 strained | At 0/390 ms the green drop covers much of the gray zigzag and can make the two look like a single small green-and-gray ornament above the sun's upper-left. This is most noticeable at 64 px. The sun's face and radiating silhouette remain readable; later phases expose the gray bend more clearly, and I cannot identify a definite competing state symbol. The concern is loss of separation between two cues in part of the motion cycle. | **needs_user_judgment** |
| #25 — cat/03 critical (control) | The green sweat visibly crosses one red arrow, but the red vertical shafts and downward arrowheads still clearly read as the critical/downward cue across the shown sizes/phases. The drop remains visibly green and the face/ears are not meaningfully obscured. The overlap does not change the sign's interpretation. | **nofix** |
| #27 — cat/03 sleeping (control) | The green sweat crosses the diagonal blue Z arrangement, including a lower/middle segment. Enough blue Z structure and the stepped progression remain visible to read sleep immediately; the largest upper Z is especially clear. The closed-eye face reinforces that reading. Contact is visible but does not meaningfully obstruct the character or convert the mark into another cue. | **nofix** |

**No confirmed candidate among these five.** The three strained cases deserve only the limited borderline label above under the user's threshold. The two controls demonstrate why alpha intersection must not be promoted automatically to a fix. A single enlarged view should not override the shown native-size reading, and four sampled phases do not establish continuous-motion perceptual failure.
